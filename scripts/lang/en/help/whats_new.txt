## Version 1.7.3

What's new:

• Keep notes made there (Settings → Piano roll, a new page with Space past the keys and Space before the start): shapes keep the notes they make past the keys and before bar 1, drawn see-through, and every tool works on them, so Gate sensitive merge can pull a shape hanging over the top key down into the one below. They're kept in the project file; Wrap them around puts them back onto the keys in exports, Domino copies and playback, Show wrapped notes shows where they land (red where they hit a note already there), and holding W shows exactly what will be exported. The status line counts them in red. See Help → Notes past the keys.

• Strum got Keys per step (how many notes of a chord move together on each step, or a range like 1 to 10 that grows along the chord) and At the shape's edges: Cut at the edges drops what the strum pushes out, Wrap back in brings it in at the other end so the block stays full. Strum a block of coloured columns with Wrap back in and the colours run through it in slanted stripes; with "Repeated colours get new channels" on, a Colours shape takes new channels each time round, so no colour repeats. See Help → Strum.

• Liquify… (in the Recolour row, beside Stir…): a brush you paint with on the piano roll that pushes the colours inside a shape around like wet paint, while the notes stay put. Push, Restore, Smooth, Twirl, Pinch (Alt: Bulge) and a Freeze mask, with Size, Density, Pressure, Rate and Smoothing for steady strokes; hold Shift for straight strokes (Shift+Ctrl snaps them to every 15°). The bends stay with the shape: change its colours underneath and they flow along. The window can hide them or set their Strength (0 to 400 %). See Help → Liquify.

• Bulge in the Stir window (beside Turn and Twist): swells the colours out from the middle like a lens, or pinches them in with a minus number, for the bulging "ball" look of op art. No colour is added or lost: they get packed tighter towards the edge. The knob goes from -200 % to +200 %; type up to ±1000 % in the box. Without an Area it bulges the whole shape around the Centre point; with one it stays inside the area, in any area shape (untick "Bulge" under "The area limits" to bulge the whole shape again). See Help → Stir.

• More bends in the Stir window (a new part that folds open under the knobs): Straight to round turns straight stripes into rays, or into rings with Turn at 90° (a Square area gives a square tunnel); Ripple makes them wavy, or rings like a water drop; Kaleidoscope mirrors one slice round the Centre point; Twist along a line wrings them like a ribbon. Each works on the whole shape or only inside the Area. See Help → Stir.

• Fold tracks… (right-click selected shapes, or the Tools menu): puts their notes on fewer tracks. In a row puts neighbour tracks together, so thin stripes of many colours become wide bands; Looping deals the tracks out in turn (1, 17, 33 … together). Overlapping notes are fixed (a tick), Keep colours keeps the picture as it is, and the piano roll shows the result before you press Fold. Pasted notes and drawn shapes both work; the result is plain notes, and every track stays on one channel. See Help → Fold tracks.

• The side panel can be made wider or narrower: drag the line between it and the piano roll, or double-click the line for the smallest width where nothing is cut. Drag it far to the right and it shuts into a strip at the window's edge; click the strip (or View → Side panel) to bring it back. Until you drag it, it now gets wider by itself where a text would be cut (some Windows 11 setups cut the end of a row). The drawer's panel can be resized too. See Help → Side panel width.

• Smaller and quicker: Spiderweb.exe is about 40 % smaller (about 70 MB instead of 116 MB), and its first start no longer waits while the speed-ups get ready. Opening, undo and redo, changing the gate and Stir on huge projects are up to 15 % faster. Running from the scripts gets the full speed with nothing extra to install.

## Version 1.7.2

Three engines: Merlin and Kestrel on the graphics card, BASSMIDI on the processor. It plays a loaded MIDI too, with your shapes on top. While stopped it makes the sound from the play line, so a heavy song can be waited out before Play. • Stir… (side panel, Recolour, beside New shuffle) bends a shape's colours round a Centre point with Turn and Twist, without adding or losing any colour: swirls and turned patterns made from the colours you drew or pasted. At the edges picks what happens to colours stirred past the edge: Escape, Wrap around (it follows round and slanted shapes too), Cut by the outline or Bend with the outline. Several stirs can go on one shape, each on its own tab. Flip colours mirrors the colours sideways or upside down while the notes stay put (sideways by time or by note order; inside an area across the whole shape or only that part, flipping it in place), and Middle puts the Centre point back. Size can count from the box or from the circle inside it. The window keeps it tidy: Area and Flip and reverse fold away, with a grey line saying what they're set to. See Help → Stir.

• Draw on Domino (Experimental; Project, beside Export MIDI; Windows, Domino 1.41b to 1.44 and the 64-bit 1.45 test builds, the 256 keys mod too): Spiderweb's drawing tools right on Domino's piano roll. Spiderweb hides behind a small floating bar, what you draw follows Domino's scroll and zoom, and each finished shape goes into Domino's current track by itself (Ctrl+Z takes it out again). Take lifts the notes selected in Domino out to change them with Spiderweb's tools, Send puts them back. A shape you sent stays a shape: while its notes are unchanged in Domino (moved or copied together is fine), selecting them shows a dashed outline and Take brings back the shape itself, curve and points included; Bake makes them plain notes for good. The step picks itself from the line's slope (Grids: Halves or your own list), and keys are picked by clicking the piano keys. A run can be edited afterwards: its step, copies above and below, note length, bends (Add anchor, Make the run straight) and Timing (On the grid, Where the line crosses, or Smooth with a Smoothing amount). See Help → Glissando.

• Gate extend… (right-click selected shapes): every note ends a little later, so it runs over into the notes after it. The amount is in % of each note's length or in ticks, and can change across the shape. See Help → Gate extend.

• Maestro (API) in Settings → Sound → MIDI out, when Maestro is installed: plays through Maestro, a loaded MIDI on its own ports. Maestro keeps its sound settings in a profile just for Spiderweb, opened with "Open Maestro's settings for Spiderweb…". See Help → Playing and listening.

• Updates from the update window: when a new version comes with a safety signature, "Update and restart" (Spiderweb.exe) or "Download and check" (running from the scripts) downloads it and checks it before anything is replaced. Nothing is downloaded until you press it. See Help → About Spiderweb.

• Gate Range (per key row) has a new Ends choice, "Exact (no leftover)", the default for new Ranges: the gates add up exactly to the shape's length, with a few notes a tick longer or shorter spread along. See Help → Inside fill (custom shapes).

• Remove overlaps (Project → Channels, under Multi channel): every shape keeps its own channel and colour, and no two notes sound on the same key at once. Earlier tracks win, or later ones; the losing note is split around the winner, or cut and stretched like Single channel. Single channel can now split around too ("Split around instead of stretching"). See Help → Channels.

• Space past the edges (Settings → General → Piano roll, off at first): room to scroll and put shapes below the lowest key, above the highest one and before the first beat, for big drawings and Stir areas that reach past the keys. The extra space is drawn darker; no notes are made there, and a shape partly out there keeps only its notes inside. See Help → View.

• Help pages with many headings have a small "Back to top" link next to each heading.

• The track list's Conductor row works like Domino's Conductor track: click it to highlight it, and Tab takes you to the Conductor pane (turned on if hidden). Draw a box on it to copy, cut, delete or drag the tempo and time and key signatures in that stretch, and paste them at the play line; Copy to Domino gives the Conductor track alone. A box reaching over the Conductor row and tracks, or a band on the bar numbers, takes the notes and the tempo together, and pasting it brings both back. Inside Keep timing parts, the row shows the tempo you drew there as purple lines. See Help → Track list.

See Help → Slides (Hz bass).

• Reverse track order (right-click a Slice with shape piece, or any pasted notes, beside New colours ▸): the piece's first colour swaps with its last, the second with the second to last, so its track order turns round on the same tracks.

• Stir's area Size can count "of the circle around" the shape's box: at 100 % a round area reaches the box's corners too, so the whole shape is inside.

• Stir's Colour sets (shapes with more than one set of 15 channels): "Stay in their set" keeps the whole pattern bending together, "Move between sets" lets notes take colours from other sets.

• Double right-click in the velocity pane and in the drawer switches between Select and the tool you used last, like on the piano roll.

• Note colours get deeper the louder a note is, like Domino's.

• Right-drag listening hears what's shown: the faint tracks behind the highlighted one too (Faint 0 % = not heard).

• Notes pasted from Domino keep their tracks and their CC events in every Channels setting, Single channel too (missing tracks are added at the bottom), and Recolour works on them in every setting.

• A tempo typed in the Conductor pane (like 252) now shows exactly that in Domino after Copy to Domino, not 251.99.

• What's new shows when each version came out and its code mark, under its name.

• Fixed: the track list's tips stayed hidden after a click until the mouse left the list.

• Fixed: zoomed far in, pressing Shift while moving a shape made it jump away from the mouse.

• Gate extend in % rounds each note's share to the nearest tick (before: always down), so a slanted line's ends are as even as its notes, also at low PPQ.

• Help → About and saved files show which build made them (its code mark and day), so two builds of the same version can be told apart.

• Playing stops one beat after the last note (before, a quarter of a bar), in any time signature; with Rendered ahead, once the sound has died away.

• Kestrel (GPU) with "Lower it by itself": a dense part that leaves notes out now keeps the same notes every time you play it. Before, it kept a few more each play from the third play on, so the sound kept changing.
