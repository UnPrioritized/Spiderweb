Right-click a curve, line, polyline or arc → Formula. [page:live|Live shape] strokes, [page:drawer|drawer] strokes, polygon sides and highlighted [page:funnel_curves|funnel curves] have it too. It has two parts, and a curve can have both at once: the Shape of the line bends the whole line, and the Pattern along the line repeats along it.
[clip:formulas-in-action]

Below, "curve" means all of these. On a polyline the pattern runs along all of it, round its corners.

## Shape of the line

The line takes a shape. One that ends where it starts (Circle, Flower, Heart, Figure 8) is as wide as from the curve's start (A) to its end (B) and sits on that line; the others (Spiral, Slow start, Fast start, S-curve, Reverse S, Quarter circle, Exponential, Logarithmic) start at A and end at B. They look round at the zoom you have when you pick them.
[clip:formulas-shapes]

Flower's sharpness changes the petals: 0 = round waves, higher = pointed tips (1 = a star with straight sides), on to thin spikes; below 0 = fatter, rounder petals with narrow dips between them.

## Pattern along the line

A pattern repeats along the line (along its shape, if it has one). These are the notes each one makes:

Loops = how many times it repeats (4.5 works too):
[clip:formulas-loops]

Height = how far it swings to each side, in keys:
[clip:formulas-height]

Curls: the line runs back over itself in little loops; "curl" over 1 makes loops, under 1 a wave.

Flatten (in the side panel under Symmetric halves, and in Custom…) presses the tops of the bumps flat, like a balloon pressed on a wall: they keep their width and only get lower (50 = flat at half their height, 100 = no bumps left). It works with every pattern, a saved one keeps it, and it stays when you pick another one. Softness says where the flat top starts: 0 = a hard cut with sharp corners, higher = rounder edges. Custom…'s preview shows the loop before it's pressed (its points are what you drag); the piano roll shows it pressed.
[clip:formulas-flatten]

## Graphs along the line

Every number of a pattern, Loops too, has a "…" button after its box, in the side panel, in Custom… and in [page:path|Copy along a path]. It opens a graph like the [page:tumours|tumour] graphs: from the start of the line (left) to its end (right), where 100 % = the number in the box. Drag the points, pick Rise, Fall, Hill or Valley, or type a formula; Flat (off) takes the graph off. A blue "× graph" next to a box means that number follows a graph.
[clip:formulas-graphs]

The Loops graph says how crowded the loops are: they bunch up where it's high and spread out where it's low, and the number of loops stays the same. At 0 % there are no loops, only the plain line. With Each piece the graph runs over every piece on its own; with Across all, once over the whole line.
[clip:formulas-graphs-loops]

Height changes smoothly along the line; the other numbers (like Sharp or Waves) change from one loop to the next. Saved patterns keep their graphs.

## Changing it

The curve you drew stays as a dashed line with its [page:curves_pen|anchors and handles]: bend it and the shape and pattern follow.
[clip:formulas-follow]

The side panel shows the selected curve's numbers (like turns, petals, height and Loops). On a joined curve, Across all runs the loops on from piece to piece and Each piece gives every piece all of them. Separate curves each get their own pattern: join them first to run one pattern across them.

[page:symmetric|Symmetric halves] (in the Formula menu under each list, in the Custom… window and in the side panel): only the first half of the formula counts, and the second half is made from it: Mirrored (arch), or Turned (S): turned half way round. A shape that ends where it starts stays closed. It works on your own formulas and saved ones too, and Save keeps it.

Remove formula takes them off (the curve you drew is left). Turn into plain curve makes what you see into ordinary anchors and handles (a line, polyline, arc or polygon becomes a curve); if its two halves match, it gets Symmetric halves.

## Custom… and your own formulas

Custom… (at the top of each list) shows every shape or pattern with its formula and a box for each of its numbers. The preview shows one loop of a pattern, or the whole shape: drag its points to change it by hand, and every loop on the curve follows (Back to formula forgets it). Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y undo and redo your changes inside the window (Cancel still puts everything back).

When its two halves match (like Wave, Bounce, S-curve or Circle), the points come as symmetric halves: moving a point moves its partner in the other half too. Double-click the line to add a point, right-click a point to remove it, Alt+drag for a sharp corner.

The grid shows the numbers (x from 0 to 1 along one loop; y in keys); the mouse wheel zooms, dragging empty space (or middle-dragging) moves around, and Fit view shows all of it again. Save puts it in your list (patterns.json in the user folder next to Spiderweb), and from then on it's in the Formula menu too. Copy formula copies it as text with the numbers from the boxes filled in, to share or keep (changes made by hand aren't in it).

Your own formula: tick Edit formula. A pattern is y of x for one loop (x goes from 0 to 1, y = how far to the side); a shape is x(t) and y(t), t going from 0 to 1 while it's drawn. Any other name in it (like height) gets its own number box. A closed shape can also be typed as one line: Type as → Polar r(t), where r is how far from the middle the shape is as it goes once round (1 = a circle; Flower can be switched to it). Its x(t) and y(t) are made from it, so it's one or the other: change x(t) or y(t) by hand and the polar line is gone. A pattern's "along =" box can move the line forward or back along the curve, in loops; going back makes it run over itself, like Curls.

Formulas copied from websites and graphing sites work as they're written, LaTeX too (\frac{2}{3}\sin\left(3t\right)): 3t, 2pi, a t and t sin(t) multiply, a leading "x =" or "r(θ) =" is left out, π × ÷ − ² ⁴ ⁻¹ √ and |t| are understood, sin^2(t) is sin(t)², sin^-1(t) is arcsin(t), sin 3t is sin(3t), ** is a power, [ ] and { } are brackets, and np. or math. before sin or cos is dropped. In a shape, θ is t, and a pasted point (x, y) fills both x(t) and y(t). Letters stuck together stay one name: "at" gets its own box (write a t or a*t for a times t); π, θ and Greek letters always stand on their own (2πt, cosθ, kθ), and sin2t is sin(2t). Written together right after / or ^, a group stays together: t/2π is t/(2π), e^-2t is e^(−2t) (1/2 t with a space is t/2). log typed is the natural log; \log pasted from a graphing site is base 10, as it was there. Full-width signs like （ ） ＝ ， work too. Websites often run t from 0 to 2pi: type that in "t from … to …" (a shape's own, usually 0 to 1) and paste the formula as it is; Polar r(t) goes once round over it. Symbols… (after Edit formula) opens a small window of math symbols you may not have on your keyboard: click one to copy it, and with Edit formula ticked it's typed into the formula too. Greek letters like δ are names like any word, each with its own number box.

Works: numbers, + - * / ^ % ( ), pi, e, sin cos tan asin acos atan sinh cosh tanh sqrt exp ln log log10 log2 abs min max floor ceil round pow, arcsin arccos arctan asinh acosh atanh sec csc cot. log is the same as ln (the natural log); use log10 for base 10.

## Other places

A stroke of a live shape (or of any placed drawing): right-click the stroke on the piano roll. Its sizes are in keys as the piano roll looks when you pick it, and grow and shrink with the shape after that. The fill follows what you see, and new strokes snap onto the ends you see.

In the drawer, a pattern's sizes are hundredths of the board, so they grow and shrink with the placed shape. Funnel curves: a shape runs from the curve's start to its wall end inside the funnel (Slow start is x²); linked curves get the same formula; Back to the default curve resets it. Curve formulas saved in older versions (curves.json) are in the [page:shapes|Shape list] now.

The velocity pane has a Formula tool too: a line with a pattern swinging around it (see [page:velocity|Velocity pane]).
