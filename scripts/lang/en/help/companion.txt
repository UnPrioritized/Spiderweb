Draw on Domino (Project, beside Export MIDI) puts Spiderweb's drawing tools right on Domino's piano roll. Spiderweb's window hides and a small bar floats over Domino instead. What you draw lands on Domino's own grid and goes into Domino as real notes. If Domino isn't running, Spiderweb starts it (the first time it asks where Domino.exe is).

It's experimental. Spiderweb works with Domino from the outside, following its window and putting notes in through Domino's own commands and clipboard. It has been tested, but not every song, setting and order of actions can be, so something could still go wrong: notes in the wrong place or missing, a step Ctrl+Z doesn't take back, or Domino closing. Save your song in Domino first and keep backups of songs that matter. The safe way always works: draw in Spiderweb, then Copy to Domino (Ctrl+Shift+C) and paste in Domino. Each start asks first, until you tick "Don't show this again".

It works on Windows only. On Linux and macOS, and when Spiderweb or Domino runs through a layer that runs Windows programs on another system, the button isn't there; Copy to Domino and pasting work as usual.

## The bar

[clip:companion-bar]

• Domino: Domino's own mouse. Clicks go to Domino, so you select, edit and play there as usual. Esc or a double right-click while drawing does this too.

• The tool button (Line, Curve, Funnel, Custom shape...): draw on Domino's piano roll with that tool, exactly like in Spiderweb. The tool you used last is picked again next time.

• Take / Send / Bake: see below. ↔ and ↕ flip the shapes selected on Domino's piano roll; with none selected there, they flip the notes selected in Domino, in one click. Stir… and Sustain… open those windows for the selected shapes.

• Spiderweb: stop drawing on Domino and bring Spiderweb's window back.

Drag the dots at the bar's left end to move it: it stays at that spot on Domino's piano roll, follows Domino's window, and hides while Domino is minimised. A short line under the buttons says what just happened. Clicking the bar never takes the keyboard from Domino.

## Drawing

The mouse wheel scrolls and zooms Domino as if you turned it over Domino itself (Shift and Ctrl too), and what you draw follows Domino's scroll and zoom. Spiderweb takes Domino's PPQ while this is on, and gives yours back when you go back to Spiderweb.

A shape you draw stays on Spiderweb's layer until you're done with it, so you can still change it (drag its points, a curve's handles...). It goes into Domino's current track, at the spot where you drew it, when you draw the next shape, go back to Domino's mouse, press Enter or Send, or press one of Domino's keys. Then it leaves Spiderweb's layer.

Glissando: while the Glissando tool is in use, or a run is selected, Domino's piano keys on the left pick the run's keys, just like Spiderweb's own piano keys: click a key to hear it and pick that note in every octave, Ctrl+click for just that key. Picked keys light up blue over Domino's keys. With any other tool, or Domino's mouse, a click there is Domino's again.

Keys: while drawing, Esc, Enter, Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y for what you did here, Delete for selected shapes and the drawing tools' letters are Spiderweb's. Every other key (Space, Ctrl+S, the arrows...) goes on to Domino.

Your project's own shapes are hidden while you draw on Domino, and come back when you go back to Spiderweb. With Single channel, overlapping notes on a key are fixed the way Single channel always does it (cut and stretched, or split around with "Split around instead of stretching"), together with the notes already in that Domino track; Domino's notes elsewhere in the track stay as they are. With Multi channel, each channel goes on its own track, from the current one down, with the channels those tracks already have; with Remove overlaps ticked, the overlaps are fixed against the notes already in those tracks too, the track order deciding which note wins; when the track list runs out, Spiderweb adds new tracks at the bottom with Domino's own Add Track (channel 10 is skipped unless "Use channel 10" is ticked). Ctrl+Z right after takes the notes out of Domino again and gives you the shape back to change; press Send to put it in again. Once you work in Domino itself, Domino's own Ctrl+Z takes over.

## Changing Domino's notes

There is one selection: Domino's own. Select notes in Domino with its select box, then press Take: they come out of Domino into Spiderweb, at the same spot, as one shape. Change it with Spiderweb's tools (flip it, Sustain…, move or stretch it), then press Send and it goes back into Domino. In Domino that is two steps to undo (the delete and the paste).

## Several tracks

Take can work on several of Domino's tracks at once: select them in Domino's track list (Tab), go back to the piano roll and press Take. Each track becomes one colour of the shape, so Stir… can mix them. Send puts every note back into the track its colour came from, whichever track is current, in Single and Multi channel alike. A track you moved in Domino in the meantime is followed; a track you deleted, and any colour that wasn't in the Take, goes to new tracks at the bottom.

Controllers (CC: volume, pan...) selected with the notes go along with the shape and back into their own tracks on Send. A selected track that holds only controllers is left in Domino as it is, and so are controllers when every note you took is a remembered shape (see below).

Take says no, and takes nothing, when Domino's Conductor track is selected too (its tempos would be taken out), or when Spiderweb can't tell which track a selected controller is on.

## Onion skin

With Domino's onion skin on (View → Onion Skin), Take also takes the notes of the tracks it shows behind the current one: the previous or next track, the tracks you picked, or the tracks of the same colour. Only the notes inside your select boxes come: drag a box in Domino's piano roll as usual, and Ctrl+drag (or Shift+drag) to add more boxes. A note counts when it starts inside a box, the same way Domino's own box picks the current track's notes. Without boxes (after Select All, for example), each group of selected notes makes its own box.

Spiderweb takes the notes out of each onion track one after another, so Domino visibly switches tracks for a moment and then goes back to yours. The other notes and the controllers in those tracks stay as they are. In Domino each track is one more step to undo; Ctrl+Z on Spiderweb's layer (with a drawing tool picked) puts every track back at once. A track whose notes can't be taken safely is left as it was, and the line under the bar names it. Tracks set to the Automatic colour never match in "same colour" mode, just as Domino shows none behind them.

## Shapes Spiderweb remembers

A shape you sent stays a shape for Spiderweb after it goes into Domino. Select all its notes in Domino and press Take, and it comes back as the shape itself, with its curve, points and handles, ready to change, wherever it is now. Notes you select that aren't part of a sent shape come out as plain notes, as before.

• Select notes of a remembered shape in Domino and an outline (a white line with orange dashes) shows around the whole shape.

• Move the shape's notes together in Domino (drag them, cut and paste them somewhere else) and Spiderweb follows. Copy the whole shape in Domino and the copy is remembered too.

• Change one of its notes on its own (move it, change its gate or velocity, delete it) and Spiderweb stops treating them as that shape. Put the note back, for example with Domino's Ctrl+Z, and the shape is back.

• Bake: select notes of remembered shapes in Domino and press Bake to make them plain notes for good. Take then gives plain notes for them too. This can't be undone.

Spiderweb keeps this in a small file next to the song with the same name plus ".spiderweb" (for example "song.dms.spiderweb"), so it's still there when you open the song again. Save the song in Domino first: until it has a file name, the shapes are remembered only while that Domino stays open. Keep the .spiderweb file next to the song if you move it; without it the song is just fine, its notes simply aren't remembered as shapes any more.

## Good to know

• It works with Domino 1.41b, 1.42, 1.43 and 1.44 (the official versions, translated ones too) and the 1.45 test builds (32-bit and 64-bit), the 256 keys mod among them (Spiderweb sees its 256 rows and draws on all of them). Spiderweb finds what it needs inside Domino by name and checks it all first: a Domino where something doesn't add up (a newer version that changed inside) is left alone, with a message, never guessed at. If Spiderweb found its way around that Domino and only the checks failed, the message offers "Try anyway": it's at your own risk (notes could land in the wrong place, or Domino could close), so save your song first. A renamed Domino (like "Domino (2).exe") is followed too.

• It doesn't work while a MIDI file is loaded in Spiderweb.

• Spiderweb only reads Domino's memory, it never writes into it: the notes go in through Domino's own commands (Copy, Paste, Delete, Undo, Add Track), so the clipboard holds the last notes sent.

• Domino can hold only one note on the same key at the same tick, so stacked notes become one there.
