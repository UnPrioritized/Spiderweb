Select one or more shapes, then right-click one: Sustain… On each key, every note is held on until the last note of its group ends, so the notes pile up on top of each other. Made for lagging out MIDI players: far more notes sound at once, while the shape looks the same.

A group is the notes on one key with no real gap between them. Small gaps, like the spacing between a spam shape's notes, are held over. Bigger gaps are holes in the shape (the inside of a ring, the space between letters) and stay empty, so the shape keeps its look.

Hold over gaps up to: Auto works out the gap from the shape's own notes (the most common gap between notes on a key, plus half of it) and shows it in ticks. Or type your own number of ticks: a bigger number than a hole holds the notes across the hole too. The number follows the PPQ, so changing the PPQ keeps the look.

Each note at most: each note is held to this many ticks long at most, instead of to the end of its group. It never goes past the group's end, and notes that are already longer stay as they are.

Stop every: the sustain stops every this many ticks, counted from the shape's first note ("starting at" moves the stops later). At a stop, every note sounding there ends, and one new note per key starts on the same tick, so the sustain starts again with no gap and the shape keeps its look. A resized shape gets more or fewer stops.

Stop at my points: stops you put yourself. Add stop puts one on the next snap line after the last stop, Clear points takes them all away. These stops stretch with the shape when it's resized. With Allow past the shape's end ticked, a stop can sit past the end: the last notes on each key are then held on until it, so the shape gets longer.

While the shape is selected, the piano roll shows these stops as lines. With the Select tool, drag a line to move its stop (it sticks to the snap lines; hold Shift to place it freely), or right-click a line to remove it. Ctrl+click and Ctrl+drag leave the lines alone.

To add a stop, right-click the shape → Add sustain stop here, or double-click the shape while the Sustain window is open. With a lot of notes and many stops there can be too many pieces: then the stops aren't put on, and the window says so.

Lengths in: show the numbers in ticks or in beats (typing 1/3 in beats works too). Drag a box (or the unit beside it) sideways to change its number.

Changes show on the piano roll at once, and the piano roll can still be used while the window is open: the window follows the selection. OK keeps the changes as one undo step, Cancel or Esc puts back what changed. Ctrl+Z steps back through the changes made in the window, on the piano roll too while it's open.

With several shapes selected, each keeps its own settings until you change that row; a half-ticked box means they differ.

The menu item has a tick while a selected shape is sustained, and the Shapes list says (Sustained). Untick Sustain notes in the window to take it off.

Sustain comes after the outline gate, Colours, Gate aware flip, glue and the Claw machine / Strum / Chop / Copy along a path pages: it only makes notes longer. Only Gate subtract comes after it.

Channels: a sustained shape keeps its overlaps in every Channels mode, even Single channel. Other shapes still fix their own overlaps, and their notes end where a sustained note on the same key starts.

Colours don't split a group: with Colours taking turns along a key, the whole row is still held, the colours piling up on top of each other.

A Hz bass and an image can't be sustained: holding a Hz bass's repeats would ruin the sound, and an image's notes are its picture.

Spiderweb's own playback can lower the voice limit when it can't keep up (if "Lower it by itself when overloaded" is ticked in Settings (⚙) → Sound), so it can sound lighter than the lag other players get from the MIDI file.
