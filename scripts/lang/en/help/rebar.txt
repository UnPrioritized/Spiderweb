Changes the bar lines without changing the sound: draw a Select box in the [page:conductor|Conductor pane] over the bars, right-click inside it → Change visual time signature…, and type the new one (6/4, 7/8…). The box takes the whole bars it touches, and each of them becomes one bar of the new signature. The notes stretch with the bars and the tempo changes by the same amount, so every note still plays at the same moment: 4/4 at 100 BPM turned into 6/4 becomes 150 BPM. After those bars, the old time signature and tempo come back, and everything later moves along. The box shows which bars it took and what the tempo becomes; with different signatures inside, each bar keeps its own length. A tempo ramp inside keeps its steps, stretched with the bars (an Interval of 24 ticks becomes 36 at 1.5 times as long, kept as that ramp's own Interval), so every note stays on its beat; where that wouldn't be a whole number of ticks, the notes on that ramp go to the tick where they sound.

Shapes inside the bars stretch with them (their gate, note tool pages, [page:sustain|Sustain], [page:extend|Gate extend] / subtract and [page:hz_bass|Hz bass] too, so a Hz bass stays in tune). A shape sticking out of the bars is cut at their edge into plain notes, each note moved exactly; Domino pastes stay one piece. [page:gliss|Glissando] runs and Hz basses with Fixed gates become plain notes too, as does anything that can't keep its sound stretched. The box counts these shapes as you type; when you press OK it first tries the change, and if more shapes would become plain notes it says so and OK becomes Change anyway. One Ctrl+Z takes the whole change back.

It isn't possible over a catch-up or a Keep timing part that reaches over the box's edge (move the box or that part first), over a mask sticking out of the bars, or with a loaded MIDI that has notes after the box's start. Shorter bars can bring notes under a tick apart. The box then says how many ("12 notes will be under a tick apart") and shows Short BPM changes for exact timing, ticked at first (it remembers your choice): those notes each keep a tick of their own, with extra BPM changes at just those spots, on top of the bars' own BPM change, so every one still plays exactly on time. Unticked, they go to the nearest tick, and notes on the same key can land together. A higher PPQ avoids both. When most notes in the bars would be under a tick apart (a 1-tick spam squashed), short BPM changes everywhere would only undo the change: the box says how many times the PPQ it needs instead ("2/4 needs twice the PPQ...") and OK puts them on the nearest tick; Cancel, raise the PPQ, and try again to keep every note on its own tick. Where a note has to land between two ticks it goes to the nearest one: the pane marks those places on the changed bars ("Notes moved by up to a tick", "Notes now under 1 tick apart", "CC moved by up to a tick") until your next change, as one band where many sit close together.
