The line stops at the end of each note, and the tone jumps at the next note. To make it slide instead, select two notes (the second one starting at or after the end of the first), right-click and pick Slide between the selected notes; or middle-click a note where the slide should start and another note where it should end. Right-click works too: a note → Start a slide here, then right-click the other note.
[clip:hz_slides-slide]

Two dots show (only while Red line is ticked at the top of the window; middle-click and Start a slide here need it too): drag them to change the slide. Middle-click a dot (or right-click it → Delete this slide) to remove the slide.

A small square sits on the slide: drag it up or down to bend the slide. It sticks where the slide is straight (Shift = free). Right-click a dot or the square to pick Single curve (one arc) or Double curve (an S: drag one way for slow ends, the other way for quick ends).

A hollow square follows the Glide curve knob while Glide is on (else it's straight); a filled one has its own bend. Double-click the square to make it follow the knob again.

A slide belongs to its two notes: notes added later don't change it.

With three or more notes selected, Slide between the selected notes makes a chain: each note slides to the next selected one. A note slides to several only when they start together (a chord after it), and several notes can slide to one. For any other pair, middle-click for each slide.

A note can also bend on its own, with the Bend tool (B): click a note to add a point to its bend line and drag it up or down. Points snap to the grid and to whole keys (Shift = free); the points at the note's two ends only move up and down. Right-click a point to remove it.
[clip:hz_slides-bend]

A small square sits half way between two points: drag it up or down to curve that piece (it sticks where the piece is straight, Shift = free; double-click it to make it straight again). The line stretches with the note, and it bends the note on top of everything else, as far as you drag it (the Pitch box's Range doesn't limit it). A slide leaves from the bent pitch and arrives at the bent pitch.

Right-click a note → Bend: Copy bend copies its bend line, Paste bend gives it to the note (or to all the selected notes, if it's one of them; the line stretches to each note), Clear bend takes it away.

Below them are ready shapes that replace the bend: Scoop up (from 2 keys below onto the note in its first 1/8 beat), Fall off (down 5 keys in its last 1/4 beat), Wobble (half a key up and down, 4 times a beat), and four that move 2 keys over the first or last quarter of the note: Rise at the end, Drop in from above, Scoop up and rise at the end, Drop in and fall at the end. They can be changed afterwards with the Bend tool.

Press R to put the shape you picked last on all the selected notes again (Scoop up until you pick one).

## Pitch tools

Right-click a note → Set Pitch… to type the key it should sit on (like C4, F#2 or 60) and its tune in cents. Its bend keeps its shape; other selected notes move by as much.

Align Note-On (N) moves a bent note to the key its bend starts on and moves the bend back by as much, so it sounds the same but sits on the row where it starts. With the Arpeggio's scale on, notes that would sound different after the move are left as they are (the status line says so).

With two or more notes selected, Join into one note (J) turns them into one bending note: from the first note's start to the last one's end, each note's pitch in turn. A gap between them keeps the pitch before it, then it jumps to the next note. The joined note keeps the first note's loudness and gate settings. Notes that overlap in time can't be joined (one note can't play two pitches at once), and neither can notes too far apart in pitch for one note's bend.

Quick detune: with the Select tool and one note selected, drag the top or bottom edge of its row up or down to spread it into detuned copies, from as far below to as far above its pitch (up to an octave each way). Dragging the edge sideways moves the note as usual, and a double-click still deletes it. Zoomed far out (small rows) there's no edge to drag: zoom in first. The drag goes in half-key steps (hold Ctrl for any cent). While dragging, the wheel or Up / Down picks how many copies (2 to 9), and Esc cancels. Each copy is a note of its own with its own tune; copies more than half a key away sit on the row nearest their pitch.

When you let go, a teal rail shows over the copies: it follows the top of the fan, and its height is the spread at that moment. Drag its end squares up or down (half-key steps, Ctrl = any cent) to make the copies start together and fan out, or close back in. Double-click the rail to add a point, drag middle points both ways (on the grid, Shift = free), right-click one to remove it, and Alt+drag between two points to curve that part. The copies bend to follow the rail. Enter (or clicking elsewhere) keeps the copies as they are; Esc puts the single note back.
[clip:hz_slides-rail]

Right-click a note → Bend → Simplify points… thins out a bend line with many points: drag the Points slider (it starts at all points, so nothing changes until you move it) and the notes show the result as you go, "14 points → 5". Fewer points keep only the biggest moves: holds and turning points stay, the rest become smooth curves. Round to semitones puts the kept points on whole keys from the note's own pitch. Points a wobble starts or ends on always stay. With several notes selected, each keeps the same share of its points. The Loudness pane's right-click has Simplify points… too, for loudness lines.

The Sketch tool (S) shapes the same bend line by hand, with curves, handles, wobbles, a box to stretch notes and the LFO window: see [page:hz_sketch|Sketch tool].
