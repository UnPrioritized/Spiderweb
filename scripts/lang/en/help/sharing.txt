Shapes can be shared as one line of text that starts with SPIDERWEB1:. Paste it anywhere as text to share it; whoever copies it can paste it into their own Spiderweb.

On the piano roll, Ctrl+C copies the selected shapes and also puts that line on the clipboard (with a curve or stroke of a [page:live|live shape] picked, it copies only that, with no line). Only the shapes go along, not the PPQ, tempo or other settings, so they look the same in any project.

Ctrl+V (or a Select double-click on empty space) pastes whichever you copied last, a shared line or shapes in Spiderweb: at the play line (or where you double-click), with the same spacing between the shapes.

For other MIDI programs: tick "Ctrl+C also copies as Spiderweb Notes" in [page:settings|Settings] → Copying, and Ctrl+C (and Ctrl+X) also puts the finished music on the clipboard in a format other programs can read: the notes as [page:files|Export MIDI] writes them, with tempo, time and key signatures, track names, colours and CC events (a pasted song's CC events only when you pick by time, with a band along the ruler or a box in the track list, as with [page:domino|Copy to Domino]). It's only useful if a program you use reads it; its makers can find how it's laid out in SPIDERWEB-NOTES-FORMAT.md, next to Spiderweb.

The status bar says when the line is longer than 2,000 characters, since some places may not take that much at once. Shapes with many points or pasted notes make longer lines.

In the [page:drawer|drawer], Export copies the whole drawing (its name, lines and coloured areas) as a line. Import adds a shared drawing to your shape library; if the name is taken, it gets a number, like "Star (2)". The drawing you're working on isn't touched.

Shared shapes can't be imported in the drawer, and shared drawings can't be pasted on the piano roll: Spiderweb says which one it is. A line that was cut off or changed is refused, so nothing half-broken gets in.

Text: if a text's font isn't installed on your PC, its letters stay as they were. The first time you change the text, Spiderweb asks, then redraws all of it in the default font (Arial on Windows).
