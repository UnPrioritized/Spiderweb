Copy (on the Domino row under Project, or Ctrl+Shift+C) puts the selected shapes' notes on the clipboard, or all notes when nothing is selected. In Domino, pick the track and press Ctrl+V, or double-click where the notes should go. Copying and pasting notes this way is tested on Windows; on Linux (Domino through Wine) it is untested.

To skip the extra step, tick "Ctrl+C also copies for Domino" in Settings → Copying: then the normal Ctrl+C (and Ctrl+X) copies for Domino too, without any questions. A very big copy is left out of it (the status line says so): use Copy for those.

The Domino start dropdown above the row sets where the notes start, for copying and pasting both:

• First note at tick 0: the first note lands right on Domino's cursor (or on the play line here).

• From the bar line: the empty space from the bar line before the first note comes along, so paste on a bar line and the notes keep their place in the bar.

Channels (under Project) set to As drawn or Single channel: everything goes into the track you paste into, on that track's channel.

Channels set to Multi channel: each channel with notes becomes a track, in channel order, packed together (channels without notes are skipped; with Settings → Note colours → Overlapping shapes keep their colours, the empty tracks between are kept so each row of channels stays in place): the first goes into the track you paste into, the next into the track below it, and so on. The pasted notes play on those tracks' own channels, and tracks that don't fit below the last one are dropped. With 10 tracks or more, Spiderweb asks first, since the 10th lands on a track that may play channel 10 (drums in most players). It doesn't ask when Use channel 10 is ticked.

Ticks are copied as they are: with a different PPQ in Domino the notes come out longer or shorter, so set the same PPQ in both (or use that on purpose). With 256 keys, notes above key 127 are left out (Domino has 128 keys), unless Settings → Copying → 256 keys (Experimental) is ticked.

Pasting works the other way: select notes in Domino, press Ctrl+C there, then press Ctrl+V here (or Paste on the Domino row). The start of what you copied (or its first note, see the dropdown) lands on the play line (snapped to the grid), like pasting in Domino, and ticks are taken as they are. With the Select tool, a double-click on empty space pastes them there instead.

Ctrl+V pastes whatever was copied last: notes copied in Domino after your last copy in Spiderweb come in as Pasted notes, a shape copied here after them pastes that shape. The Paste button always takes the notes copied in Domino.

The notes of every copied track become one shape (Pasted notes). Its controllers and other events (CC, pitch bend, program changes…) come along: the Shapes list says "(with CC)". They move and stretch with the shape, a sideways flip mirrors them, and Copy / Export to Domino give them back exactly as they were. Export MIDI and playback send them on each track's channel. Pasted notes keep their tracks in every Channels setting, just like Multi channel: Single channel only removes overlaps on each track. Pasted with a track highlighted in the track list, the copied tracks land from that row down just as they were copied (gaps too), and their events go along on each track's channel. Copying works like Domino: shapes picked by clicking or with a Select box copy (Ctrl+C, Copy, Duplicate, Ctrl+drag) their notes only; pick them with a band along the ruler, or a box in the track list, and their CC events come too. Moving a shape, or Ctrl+X and paste, always takes them along. Nothing hides: the track list marks those tracks with CC, and the piano roll's ruler names each event in its CC row (under the tempo changes), like "Volume 100" or "Pan 64".

Tempo and signatures: copy Domino's Conductor track along with the notes (or alone) and its tempo changes, time signatures and key signatures go into the Conductor pane. When the pane already has its own, Spiderweb asks what to do: Replace that stretch, Add among them, or Notes only. Copy here takes only the notes, so they land on the track you picked in Domino. The tempo comes along only after Ctrl+A (everything picked): then a Conductor block comes first, so pick the Conductor track before pasting. To copy just the tempo, click the Conductor pane, pick what to copy and press Copy to Domino (or Ctrl+C with "Ctrl+C also copies for Domino" ticked), then paste it on Domino's Conductor track. See Conductor pane. Each note remembers its track: with Multi channel every track gets a channel of its own (even tracks that never overlap), with Single channel all of them go on one channel and overlaps are removed.

To freeze a shape, you don't need to copy it to Domino and back (that can move its notes to other channels): right-click it → Turn into plain notes. It becomes pasted notes and every note keeps its channel.

Move, flip, turn, stretch or skew the shape like a custom shape: the notes go with its box. The notes keep their own velocities until you change the shape's velocity (in the panel or the velocity pane).

Export (on the Domino row) saves every note as a Domino song file (.dms), next to the MIDI file in the Output file box, with the same name (spiderweb.mid → spiderweb.dms). Open it in Domino like any song. It can hold a PPQ up to 65535, where a MIDI file stops at 32767. The tracks, channels, tempo and signatures come out like Export MIDI; notes above key 127 are left out (unless Domino 256 keys is ticked, as above). Notes longer than 65535 ticks are cut to 65535, the longest gate Domino can work with (Copy does the same).
