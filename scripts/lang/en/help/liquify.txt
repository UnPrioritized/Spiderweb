Liquify… (side panel, Deform row, beside [page:stir|Stir…] and [page:warp|Warp…]) is a brush you paint with on the piano roll. It pushes the colours inside a shape around (or bends the shape itself: see "Bending the shape itself" below), like a finger through wet paint, the way the Liquify tool of photo editing programs bends a picture. The notes themselves never move or change length: only which colour (channel) each note has changes. No colour is added or blended: every note takes the colour of the note the brush pulled into its place.
[clip:liquify-brush]

The bends are kept with the shape and worked out again after every change. Change the colours underneath (Recolour, Stir, Colours) and they flow along the same bends; move or stretch the shape and the bends go with it. Colour settings work in the order they were added: a Liquify added after a Stir or a [page:warp|Warp] bends their colours, one added before them is bent by them.

## Brushing

Open Liquify…, then drag over a selected shape on the piano roll. The circle shows the brush; the colours change live while you drag, exactly as they stay when you let go. Each stroke is one undo step (Ctrl+Z). Pressing on another shape picks it and brushes it (a shape is never moved while the window is open); pressing on empty space works as usual.

The window follows whichever shape is selected. With several shapes selected, a stroke bends each one on its own.

## The tools

Push: pushes the colours along the way you drag.

Restore: brushes the bends back towards none, slowly, where you paint. Restore All (the button) takes every bend off at once.

Smooth: rounds off sharp kinks in the bends without undoing them.

Twirl: swirls the colours round the brush while you hold the mouse still or drag. Left / Right picks the way round.

Pinch: squeezes the colours in towards the brush's middle. Hold Alt for Bulge: swells them out.

Freeze: paints a see-through red mask over spots the other tools must leave alone (partly red = partly left alone). Hold Alt to Thaw (take the mask away). Freeze all, Thaw all and Invert work on the whole shape; "Show freeze mask" hides the red without changing anything. Freezing never changes a colour by itself.

Holding Alt while brushing always gives the opposite: Twirl turns the other way, Pinch bulges, Freeze thaws.

## Brush settings

Size: the brush's size in pixels on the screen (zooming doesn't change it).

Density: how hard the brush's edge is. Higher = the full push reaches further out (the dashed inner circle), lower = it fades out softly from the middle.

Pressure: how strongly each step of the brush pushes.

Rate: how fast a held brush keeps working. Twirl, Pinch, Restore, Smooth and Freeze keep working while you hold the mouse still; every tool, Push too, keeps going while you hold a straight stroke's second click. 0 = only while moving.

Smoothing: the brush trails behind the mouse on a string, so shaky hand movements come out as smooth strokes. The higher, the longer the string (a line from the brush to the mouse shows it). When the mouse rests, the brush catches up with it.

At the edges: what is pulled in where the brush pushes colours past the shape's edge, as in Stir: Escape, Wrap around, Repeat the colours, Bounce back, Bounce back, edge twice, Bend or Cut. The [page:edges|At the edges] page explains each one, with pictures.

## Straight strokes

A straight stroke takes two clicks: Shift+click where it starts (a dashed circle stays there), then Shift+click where it ends. While you hold Shift, a line with arrows shows the way it will push. Keep holding the second click and the same stroke goes on and on, pushing further the longer you hold (as fast as Rate says). The next straight stroke starts with a new first click. Add Ctrl (Shift+Ctrl+click) to snap the line to every 15° (flat, upright, 45° and in between). Esc forgets the start.

## Bending the shape itself

"Moves" at the top of the Liquify window picks what the brush bends (how Stir, Warp and Liquify fit together: [page:deform|Deform: Colours or Shape]). Colours (the start) pushes the colours, as everything above says. Shape bends the shape itself where you brush, and its notes are made again as you brush. Notes may go past the shape's box, and brushing outside the box pushes the shape out there too. Shapes without a Liquify yet take the window's choice with their first stroke. In Properties a Liquify set to Shape is called "Liquify ◇" and is listed at the top.

Shape bends always run first, before every colour step: the [page:stir|Stir] tabs set to Shape, then the [page:warp|Warp], then the Liquify. The brush bends exactly where its circle touches, like the Liquify of image editors: nothing outside the circle moves, and the same strokes always give the same shape.

"Cut notes where bent" (under Moves, ticked at first) bends a drawn shape note by note: its notes are made first, then each one bends like a tiny box, cut into pieces only where the brush moves part of it to another key, so Fill rows, Spam and patterns bend along like a picture.

Unticked, only the outline bends and the notes are laid out again inside it: then pushing inside a filled shape changes nothing (the window says so in orange), so brush across the outline. Pasted notes and plain notes are always cut (the tick is greyed for them). Shapes without a Liquify yet take the window's tick with their first stroke.

Where a push squeezes notes together they bunch up, and behind it they spread apart. A very strong push can make the outline cross itself; where the shape then overlaps itself, "Overlaps cancel out" leaves a hole, as with any shape drawn that way.

Switching Moves on a shape you've already brushed asks first: the brush's bends can't be carried from one kind to the other, so they're taken away (the freeze mask stays). Ctrl+Z brings them back.

"Colours bend too" (beside Moves, ticked at first) is for the unticked way: it keeps the shape's colours on the notes they were on, so a Colours pattern or coloured areas bend along with the shape. Cut notes always keep their own colours, so it's greyed while "Cut notes where bent" is ticked. At the edges only chooses colours, so it's greyed for Shape; Freeze, Restore, Smooth and Strength work as always. On very big pastes (a million notes) each update while brushing takes about a second.

## Strength and Show the bends

In the Liquify window, untick "Show the bends" to hide a shape's bends without losing them; tick it again and they come back the same. Strength (0 to 400 %) pushes the same bends less or further: 0 shows none, 200 % pushes twice as far. Brushing a hidden Liquify shows it again.

## Big shapes

On shapes with millions of notes, a shape's first stroke takes a moment (its colours are made ready once), and after letting go the new notes take a moment to arrive (you can keep working meanwhile). While dragging only the notes whose colour changes are worked out, so the brush stays live.

## Edits that change notes

Some colours depend on the other notes in the shape: Stir, Warp and Liquify take the colour of the nearest note, Per chord and Per run follow their group, Shuffle changes a colour that would sound together with another, and Shuffle and Clumps count from the shape's first note and lowest key. So editing notes ([page:glue|Glue], [page:chop|Chop], [page:claw|Claw machine], [page:strum|Strum], [page:trim|Gate trim], [page:subtract|Gate subtract], [page:extend|Gate extend], [page:sustain|Sustain], [page:fill_gaps|Fill gaps]...) can change colours elsewhere in the shape, and in Single channel where overlapping notes end. To keep the colours as they are, use Turn into plain notes first.

Bends can never be perfectly smooth: MIDI notes sit on whole keys and whole ticks. See [page:deform#How exact a bend can be|Deform: Colours or Shape].
