The OSC tab of the synth window sets the sound the way most synths do. Turn a knob by dragging up / down (Shift = fine), with the mouse wheel or the arrow keys, or type a value in the box under it; hold the right mouse button on it to point it at the mouse; middle-click puts it back to the start. Most knobs make the effects' lines (Volume, the waveforms, Octave below, Pitch, Vibrato, Tremolo, Sweep, Wah, Slant, Groups, Off pitch, Noisy), so you can see them on the Draw tab and in the Hz bass window too, and they're in your MIDI. OSC B, the Mode knobs, Key track, the Timing of Vibrato and Tremolo, Voice and Arpeggio are settings of their own, not lines.
[clip:hz_osc-tab]

The light on each box's header is lit while that box changes the sound. Click the light or the box's name to switch the box off (bypass): its name goes grey and it does nothing, but its knobs are kept, and you can still turn them; click again to switch it back on.

Volume: Attack = how long the sound takes to get loud after you press a key, Decay = how long it then takes to drop to the Sustain level, Sustain = how loud it stays while you hold the key, Release = how long it fades out after you let go. Times are in beats. The picture under the knobs shows the envelope: the blue part is while the key is held, and a dot runs along it while a key sounds.

OSC A: the Waveform and how much of it is used (Shape), and Octave below (every other wave softer, so the tone an octave lower comes in); the picture shows two waves' notes, one bar each, as tall as it hits.

OSC B: a second oscillator. Click its light or name to switch it on: every note you placed is then played a second time, with OSC B's own Waveform, Shape and Mode, tuned by Octave, Semi (keys) and Fine (cents) from the note, as loud as its Level. Everything else (Volume, Pitch, the LFOs, Tone, Character, Voice, Arpeggio and the FX tab) works on both.

Plays on: Every key = every key of the shape plays both oscillators (about twice the notes); Split keys = the keys take turns, one OSC A, the next OSC B (no extra notes).

While OSC B is on, OSC A's light switches OSC A's own notes on and off (to hear OSC B alone), as in most synths. With Fine, OSC B's notes get alternating gates, even with Fixed gates, so the few cents are heard.

Mode (in OSC A and OSC B) changes the shape of each wave one more way. Only the picked mode's knobs are shown.

FM = a fast wobble pushes the wave (metal or bell sounds); Time makes it die away after each note starts.

Pulse width = only the first part of each wave sounds, and Rate can make it open and close.

Sync = the wave starts over faster inside each wave: a hard, screaming sound.

Growl = every other wave lands a bit late: a lower tone under it.

Bitcrush = the notes moved onto a coarse grid: rough and gritty.

Pitch: each note starts Amount keys up (or down) and glides to its own tone in Time. Range = how far the Pitch line and note bends can go (1 to 48 keys); turning it keeps the line's shape, so the whole bend gets bigger or smaller.

Vibrato and Tremolo (the LFOs, as synths call them) wobble the tone and the loudness: Rate = how many times a beat, Depth = how much. Delay = how long each note stays plain before the wobble starts, Rise = how long the wobble then takes to grow to full Depth.

Timing (in each LFO box) = how the Rate knob moves: Free = any speed; Straight = only note lengths, in step with the beat (1/4 = once a beat, 1/8 = twice); Triplets = 3 in the time of 2 of those (1/8T); Dotted = one and a half times as long (1/8D). The note length shows beside the Rate, and each wheel or arrow step goes to the next one.

Tone:
• Sweep makes one part of the shape's keys loud (Start) and can move it to End in Time after each note starts.
• Key track moves it up for higher notes (down below 0), counted from the Hz bass's own tone.
• Wah = loud and quiet stripes over the keys.
The picture shows which keys are loud over time (darker = louder, the highest keys at the top).

Character: Slant, Groups, Off pitch and Noisy make the keys hit at different spots of the wave, which changes the colour of the tone; the picture shows eight keys' notes over four waves.

Macros (the strip over the OSC and FX tabs): four big knobs that each turn several knobs at once, e.g. one knob that moves the Sweep and adds Vibrato together. Drag a macro's name onto any knob and hold it there for half a second (the knob gets a coloured outline), then let go to link them. Passing over other knobs on the way links nothing.
[clip:hz_osc-macros]

The knob keeps its own setting and the macro adds to it: a coloured ring round the knob shows how far it goes with the macro at 100 %, and a dot shows where the sound has it now. Drag the ring up or down to change how far; dragged on down past 0, the macro turns the knob the other way. Let go at 0 = unlinked.

Click a macro to pick it: the knobs show the picked macro's rings. Right-click its name to unlink knobs. A macro is one setting for the whole sound, saved with presets; it doesn't move during a note.

MOD tab: things that move knobs over time, during each note.
LFO 3 and LFO 4 go up and down over and over, with their own Shape and Rate. Plays: Free = running on from the shape's start, Each note = starting over at each note, Once = one wave from each note's start, then staying at its end.
ENV 2 and ENV 3 rise over Attack from each note's start, fall over Decay to Sustain, stay there while the note is held, and fall to 0 over Release after it.
Velocity = each note's own loudness (for Arpeggio steps: the step's Loudness).
Note = how high each note is.
[clip:hz_osc-mod]

To link one, drag its name from the strip over the OSC and FX tabs onto a knob, as with a macro. Its ring shows how far it moves the knob at its top (click its name to see its rings; drag the ring to change how far, down to 0 = unlinked; right-click its name to unlink knobs).

These knobs can move:

• Shape, Octave below, Volume Attack, Decay, Sustain and Release, Pitch Amount and Time, Vibrato Rate (its wave goes faster or slower from where it is, no jumps), Depth, Delay and Rise, Tremolo Rate, Depth, Delay and Rise.
Moving an envelope's time speeds up or slows down the part that's playing. A line drawn by hand on the Draw tab keeps its times.

• Sweep Start / End and Time, Key track, Wah, Slant, Groups, Off pitch, Noisy, Blend, Detune, Random start, Glide and Curve (these two and Random start read once, as each note starts).

• Arpeggio Speed (the run goes faster or slower from where it is), Gate and Swing (read as each step starts; an envelope, Velocity or Note moving them follows the note held longest).

• OSC B's Shape, Level, Octave, Semi and Fine (Octave and Semi move in whole octaves and keys, as their knobs).

• Each Mode's first knob (FM Depth, Pulse Width, Sync Amount, Growl Amount, Bitcrush Amount) and Time (FM, Sync), Pulse Rate and FM Ratio.

• LFO 3 and LFO 4 Rate (on the MOD tab: drag another box's name onto it; an LFO can't move its own).

• On the FX tab: Chorus Depth and Rate, Flanger Rate, Depth and Mix, Echo Time (each note's echoes are spaced by the Time at the moment that note plays) and Fade, Reverb Length (read as each note ends), Scatter and Level, Compressor Threshold, Ratio, Attack, Release and Gain.
The FX tab's effects work on the whole sound, so an envelope, Velocity or Note moving them follows the newest note (as in many synths).

Sweep and Shape move only while the Sweep is on and a waveform is picked. A knob moves only while it does something: Tremolo Depth needs a Tremolo Rate, Key track the Sweep, Blend 3 voices or more, Detune 2, a Mode's knob that Mode.

The Links list on the MOD tab shows every link: its source, the knob it moves, how far (Amount, below 0 = the other way) and Both ways (the knob swings below and above where it's set, instead of only going up from it). + Add link adds one; ✕ removes one.
