Tick Conductor pane (top, or View) to show or hide it. The pane under the piano roll (below the velocity pane) holds what the MIDI's conductor track holds: the tempo line, time signatures and key signatures. Drag the bar above it to resize it.

With no tempo points the song plays at 120 BPM, the MIDI standard. The tempo at the start is the point at bar 1: click there with the Line tool and type its BPM in the box below the tools. Projects saved with another BPM open with a point at bar 1 at that BPM.

## Drawing the tempo

Unlike many MIDI programs, the tempo here is a line you draw, like velocities: points where the BPM is set, and parts between them that hold, glide or curve.

Line: a click adds a held step. Over a held tempo it holds until the next point; inside a ramp it is a short dip until the ramp's next step, then the ramp goes on as before. A click right above or below a point sets that point's BPM instead.
[clip:conductor-click]

Above, zoomed in on a ramp: one click made a dip. The ramp's steps before and after it are the same as before.

Drag to draw a line. It replaces only its own stretch: the tempo before it stays as it was, a ramp after it goes on from where it was, and a held tempo after it takes the line's end BPM until the next point. While you drag, the pane already shows the result. The new line keeps squares at its ends and a round handle in the middle until Enter, Esc or a click elsewhere: drag a square to move that end, the handle to bend the line. Ctrl+Z takes the whole line away.
[clip:conductor-line]

Formula: drawn like Line, but the part follows the formula in the box next to it (x goes from 0 at its first point to 1 at the next).

New points sit on whole ticks (no grid), or on a tempo point right next to them. Points and line ends you move afterwards snap onto note starts and ends nearby and onto other tempo points. Hold Shift for whole ticks only. BPM snap rounds the BPM (Off for any BPM).

A click on the beat where a curved part arrives (or within one Interval step of it) makes a jump: the curve arrives there, and the tempo goes on from the second point at the same moment.

Double right-click switches between Select and the last drawing tool.

## Picking and changing

Select: click a point, catch-up or part to pick it (the play line goes there too). Drag on empty space for a Select box over that whole time; Ctrl+drag adds another box. Drag inside the box to move what it picked in time; Ctrl+drag copies it, and the pane shows the result while you drag. A copied catch-up that would land inside another catch-up is left out. Ctrl+A picks everything.

Drag a point to move it. Double-click a point to type its BPM; the row under it shows where the point sits as bar:beat:tick (e.g. 5:3:240), and typing a new place there moves the point. Or use the BPM box below the tools (drag its word like any number box). Delete removes the picked ones. Right-click gives a menu (right-click never deletes).

A picked part shows its settings below the tools:

• Part curve: Hold, then jump / Line / S-curve / Formula. Drag the round handle to bend a line; double-click it for straight again, or right-click it for Straight line and S-curve.

• The dropdown beside it: Linear adds the same BPM every beat; Exponential grows by the same share every beat, good for huge spikes.

• Keep timing: see below.

Ctrl+C copies the picked stretch, Ctrl+X cuts it, Ctrl+V pastes it at the play line (a Select double-click on empty space pastes there). A paste replaces only that stretch; the tempo after it stays as it was. These keys work on the pane while it was the last thing you clicked.

Interval: curved parts change the tempo every this many ticks (24 like many MIDI programs; 1 = every tick). The pane draws the steps the file will have.

Graph: Linear shows the BPM evenly from 0; Exponential doubles the BPM with every step up, so huge spikes and very slow stops fit on one screen. Fit shows the whole line. The bold number on the left is the tempo at the piano roll's left edge. The wheel over the numbers on the left zooms, Shift+wheel or a drag there scrolls. Where the tempo goes higher or lower than the pane shows, a small arrow at its top or bottom edge points to it: click the arrow to scroll there.
[clip:conductor-arrows]

Above: the 600 BPM stop is above the view and the 40 BPM one below it.

To draw a jumpy tempo that follows the melody for you, make a Select box and right-click → Fill with tempo jumps…. See Help → [page:fill_tempo|Fill with tempo jumps].

The ruler above the piano roll shows each tempo change ("120 → 60", "60 ↗ 180"); rest the mouse on one for the exact BPMs. Inside a Keep timing part the tempo you hear follows in purple brackets ("120 → 60 (120)"), and changes of its heard tempo line show in purple. The number in the pane's top row is the exact tempo at the play line, with the heard tempo after it in purple brackets where the two differ.
[clip:conductor-ruler]

## Catch-up

A catch-up makes up for time: after a slow (or fast) stretch, the tempo runs faster (or slower) until the music is back where it would have been at the tempo heard before it.

Right-click → Add catch-up BPM here, then two drags: first over the time it counts (the yellow area), then from there to where the catch-up should stop (the teal area). With a Select box kept, the box is the yellow area at once. Esc or right-click cancels.
[clip:conductor-catch]

Above: 120 BPM, a stop at 60 for one beat, then the catch-up runs at 140 BPM until the music is back on time when the line returns to 120.

Drag the catch-up line to change how long the slow part holds, and the edges of the yellow and teal areas to change them (right-click an edge to set it back to automatic). An edge you set stays put when you drag the line past it: a thick line shows where the area comes back to when the line returns; right-click it → Forget this edge to drop it. Shapes: Flat, Fast, then slow, Slow, then fast, Up and down, Drawn or Formula; Fast, then slow and Slow, then fast have a round handle to bend them (double-click it, or right-click → Its own curve again, to undo the bend). Drawn opens a small window where you draw the shape yourself with points and round bend handles, starting from the shape it had before (Graph… opens it again). You only draw how hard it pushes at each moment: Spiderweb still works out the BPM, so it always lands on time. The bar shows a catch-up's shape when you click its line, when a Select box holds it, or when the play line stands inside its yellow or teal area (while stopped, with nothing picked).

The line below the tools says the BPM the catch-up needs. In red it can't work: "Not possible" says why (for example "catch-up BPM too high"), and resting the mouse on the red area tells you what to change.

## Keep timing

Keep timing lets a part show one tempo while the notes sound at another. Pick a part and choose in the Keep timing dropdown:

• Keep the audio: the notes keep the time they had at the tempo heard just before the part. The tempo you draw only changes how fast the piano roll scrolls in other programs; the music sounds the same.

• Keep the visual: the other way round. The tempo line stays what the file shows, and the purple line is the tempo the notes sound at.

Either way the export moves the notes in ticks to make it exact. Switching between the two swaps the lines. Off turns Keep timing off for the picked part only: the parts next to it keep it. The part after the last point can be picked too (to the song's end). Keep timing doesn't work inside a catch-up's yellow area.
[clip:conductor-keep]

Above, Keep the audio: the file shows 60 BPM in the purple part, so the piano roll scrolls at half speed in other programs, but the notes still sound at 120, the dotted purple line.

Draw: Visual tempo / Heard tempo (in the pane's top row) picks which line the tools draw. With Heard tempo, the tools draw the purple line, and everything outside the Keep timing parts is faded. A line drawn across several parts keeps only what lands inside them. Where the sound jumps at a part's end, a dotted upright line joins the two tempos.
[clip:conductor-heard]

Above, Keep the visual with Heard tempo picked: the file keeps showing 120 BPM (the faded line), while the music speeds up from 120 to 240 along the purple line. Spiderweb moves the notes in the file so they sound that way.

Turning Keep timing on puts a point at the part's end at the heard tempo's BPM there, so the sound goes on smoothly. It follows the heard line's end until you change it.

A Keep timing part drawn faster than the tempo before it gives the MIDI file more ticks a second than the piano roll has. Tick Finer gates in Keep timing parts in the Hz bass window's Settings… and that Hz bass is made on those finer ticks there, so it buzzes less, most of all on high notes. Only the exported file changes: the piano roll and playback stay on the project's ticks.

Fit bar lines (top row, on at first): when Keep timing moves notes, the export adds time signatures so the bar lines in MIDI programs still sit on the bars you hear.

## What Keep timing can't do

A MIDI file only has whole ticks, so Keep timing has limits. The pane marks each spot in red or orange; rest the mouse on the text for more. When a spot is out of view, a small arrow at the part's edge points to it: click it to scroll there. A higher PPQ helps with all of them.

• Notes under 1 tick apart (red): at the tempo you drew, two notes would land less than one tick apart. There the file keeps a faster tempo instead, so the sound stays right but other programs show a different tempo for that bit. An orange dashed line marks each spot.

• CC moved by up to a tick (orange): CC messages (Hz bass CC lines, pasted CC) that fall between ticks move to the nearest tick so the tempo can stay. Notes are never moved this way.

• Bar line moved a little (orange): Keep timing made a bar a length no time signature fits. Fit bar lines writes the nearest one instead, so other programs show that bar line a little off, and the next bars put it back. The sound is still right.

• No time signature fits this bar (red): the same, but with no notes after it to put the bar line back, so the bar is left without one. Other programs show the bar lines off there; the sound is still right.

• Inside a catch-up's yellow area Keep timing is off: the catch-up needs the real tempo there.

• Paste from Domino and Load MIDI bring in only the tempo the file shows; the heard line starts flat.

## Time and key signatures

Right-click in the pane → Add time signature here… or Add key signature here…. Each starts on the bar line at or before the click and shows as a short line from the pane's top edge ("3/4", "Ab Maj"). Double-click one to change it, or right-click it to change or remove it (the first time signature can only be changed). The ruler shows them next to the bar number: "5 (3/4, Ab Maj)".
[clip:conductor-signatures]

Bar lines, bar numbers and the Bar snap follow the time signatures everywhere, the Hz bass window too. A time signature always starts a new bar: one left in the middle of a bar moves back to that bar's start.

## Change visual time signature

To change the bar lines without changing the sound (6/4 instead of 4/4, notes and tempo stretched together): draw a Select box over the bars, right-click inside it → Change visual time signature…. See [page:rebar].

## Export and Domino

Export MIDI, Copy to Domino and Export to Domino write the tempo line and the signatures, using only tempo values Domino keeps exactly. Copy to Domino takes them only after Ctrl+A (everything picked), or from the track list with a band on the bar numbers or a box reaching over its Conductor row; then they come first, so pick the Conductor track in Domino before pasting. To copy only the tempo: draw a box on the track list's Conductor row, or click this pane and pick what to copy, and press Copy to Domino (or Ctrl+C with "Ctrl+C also copies for Domino" ticked), then paste it on Domino's Conductor track. Built-in playback follows the tempo line too.

Paste from Domino brings in the tempo and signatures of the copied Conductor track. When the pane already has its own, Spiderweb asks: Replace that stretch, Add among them, or Notes only.

A Hz bass stays in tune where the tempo changes under it. When the tempo under it changes later, the Hz bass window and the panel say so in orange: press Update Hz bass.
