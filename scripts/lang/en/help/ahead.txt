Rendered ahead (in the MIDI out list) makes the sound into audio a little ahead of the play line, in small pieces, and plays it from there. Since nothing has to sound at the very moment it's played, every note can ring, even in the densest walls, and the sound never breaks up or gets "Overloaded". Pick it in Settings (Help menu) → Sound → MIDI out. It plays your soundfonts: the same stacked list as Built-in BASSMIDI (see Playing and listening).

## Playing

Play waits a moment first, until the first 5 seconds from the play line are made (less if the song ends sooner), so the start sounds just like a replay. Meanwhile the right end of the status line says what it's doing: "putting the soundfont on the graphics card…" (the first Play with Merlin or Kestrel), then "making the first seconds… 2.1 / 5". While it plays, making goes on in the background, always keeping Render ahead seconds ahead of the play line.

While playing, the part not made yet shows a very faint grey over the piano roll, and clears as it's made. The right end of the status line says how much is made ahead of the play line, in seconds, of Render ahead: "Audio buffer: 12.4 / 60" ("(to the end)" once the rest of the song is made). Stop and Play again from the same place: what's already made is played at once.

Edit while playing (draw, move, delete, change velocities) and only the changed stretch turns grey and is made again; playing goes on. A tempo change makes it all again from the play line.

Playing stops once the sound has died away, not a beat after the last note.

While stopped, the sound is made from the play line too (Make ahead while stopped, on at first): once the play line rests half a second, making starts there, up to Render ahead seconds, with the faint grey over what's not made yet. So a heavy song can be waited out: when the grey is gone, Play starts at once. Moving the play line makes from the new place; what was already made elsewhere is kept for a while, so going back plays at once too.

A loaded MIDI plays too, with your own shapes on top (muted tracks are left out). Changing it while playing (muting a track, Track Properties, Remove track, Extract, the Conductor pane) makes everything again from the play line; playing goes on. If the MIDI file itself changes on disk, playing stops and says so (load it again). Starting or jumping into the ring right after a heavy wall takes longer the first time (up to a minute): the notes still ringing there are worked out from the wall before it, so they sound as they should. Once made, going back there plays at once.

Right-drag listening, piano keys and the Hz bass window's keys can't be made ahead (the mouse decides them as you go): they sound through Built-in BASSMIDI, with the same soundfonts.

## Engine

The Engine row picks what makes the sound:

• Merlin (graphics card): the fastest, and every note is played, nothing is left out. The first Play takes a few seconds while your soundfont is sent to the graphics card.

• Kestrel (graphics card): the same synth as Kestrel (GPU) playback, with its own settings here. Exact, but much slower than Merlin.

Merlin doesn't play controllers exactly yet: volume, expression, pan, sustain pedal and CC lines other than pitch bends (for example an Hz bass's volume or pan lines, or a loaded MIDI's). Unticked (at first), "Use Kestrel where Merlin isn't exact" lets Merlin make them anyway: fast, but a little off. Ticked, songs with them are made with Kestrel, and the status line says so.

Merlin can't use more than one soundfont yet either: with several in the list, Kestrel always makes the sound, and a line under the tick says so.

• BASSMIDI (processor): works on any PC. Slower to make in busy parts.

Merlin and Kestrel need 64-bit Windows or Linux and a fairly recent graphics card with an up-to-date driver (see Kestrel (GPU) playback).

## Settings

Settings (Help menu) → Sound, while Rendered ahead is picked:

• Render ahead: how far ahead of the play line the sound is made, 5 to 300 seconds (30 to start). More = fewer waits in busy parts, but more memory.

• Slow down to keep up (off at first): ticked, when the play line catches up with the grey, playing waits there until the sound is ready, then goes on (it stutters). Off, the play line keeps the song's speed, a part not made in time stays silent, and making jumps ahead to just past the play line.

• Lower it by itself when falling behind (Kestrel and BASSMIDI, off at first): when the sound made ahead runs low, the next pieces are made with fewer voices so they're ready sooner. Those pieces are made again at the full voices later, while they're still ahead of the play line. With Slow down to keep up ticked too, this comes first.

• Graphics card: the same setting as Kestrel's: changing one changes both.

• Max voices (Kestrel) or Voice limit (BASSMIDI): the most notes that can ring at once. More = fuller busy parts, but each piece takes longer to make.

• Block size, Interpolation, Velocity threshold: as in Kestrel (GPU) playback.

• Limiter: keeps very loud parts from clipping, over the whole sound (no jumps where pieces meet). Balanced first evens out long loud parts a little, then catches the peaks; Brickwall only catches the peaks; Off lets them clip. Ceiling = the loudest it lets through, in dB. It's Kestrel's own limiter, so the sound matches Kestrel's renders.

• DC blocker (on at first): takes out the slow drift away from the middle that millions of stacked notes add up to, before the limiter. Without it the limiter turns the music down along with that drift: a dense wall's end then sounds dull, with a noisy tail after it.

Render ahead, Slow down to keep up and Lower it by itself work at once, even while playing. The Engine and the other settings are used from the next Play.
