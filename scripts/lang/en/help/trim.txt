Select one or more shapes, then right-click one: Trim shape… (also in the Tools menu; it used to be called Gate trim). Mode picks what it does:

• Trim rows: each row of touching notes on a key is cut down to a share of its whole length.
• Wrapped slices: the notes are cut into curved slices, as if the shape were wrapped round a ball or a can, and each slice gets its own colour.

The notes change on the piano roll while you try settings. OK keeps the change (one Ctrl+Z takes it back); X or Esc puts the notes back as they were. Reset puts the shown mode's settings back to where they start.

## Trim rows

Type how much stays in the Keep box (or drag the slider), then pick which end stays. Reset goes back to 100 %, where it changes nothing.
[clip:trim-keep]

Every row is trimmed by the same share of ITS OWN length, so a long row loses more ticks than a short one. A filled circle at 25 % from the left becomes a crescent: the left edge stays, and the new right edge follows the same curve, closer in.

The notes are not squeezed: the notes inside the part that stays keep their lengths, the note where the cut falls is cut there, and the rest of the row goes. For example, three touching notes of 60, 40 and 70 ticks make a row of 170 ticks; at 50 % from the left, 85 ticks stay: the 60 tick note, and 25 ticks of the 40 tick note.

The dropdown after the box picks how the number counts. "% of each row" takes up to 3 decimals (12.345); the slider moves in whole percents. "ticks of each row" makes every row keep the same number of ticks (any whole number from 1 up), so a circle becomes a crescent of even thickness; a row shorter than that stays whole, and the slider is greyed.

Count from: Left keeps the start of each row (the left part), Right keeps its end (the right part).

A gap on a key, even of 1 tick, starts a new row, and each row is trimmed on its own. Notes of every colour on a key count together.

Like the [page:claw|Claw machine], the trim is a page kept with the shape: moving or reshaping it trims the new notes the same way. Each time the window opens it starts a new page (+); click a tab on top to change one the shape has, or the small × beside a tab to take it off. Pages work one after another, in the order they were added, so a trim can come before or after a [page:chop|Chop] or a [page:strum|Strum].

For a shape that wipes away or grows in (like the moon's phases), copy it a few times and give each copy its own share: 100 %, 75 %, 50 %, 25 %.

## Wrapped slices

Every row is cut into slices, and slice by slice the colours go round. A filled circle becomes a striped beach ball whose stripes meet in two points. Any shape works: a triangle fans out from its tip, a square gets straight stripes like a can. The points come only from the outline getting narrower; nothing is added. No note is lost or moved: notes are only cut where a slice ends.

• Slices: how many slices each row is cut into. Slices thinner than a tick, or than a key where they run sideways, can't show and vanish.
• Colours: how many colours the slices go round (15 at most, 16 with "Use channel 10"). Slice 1 keeps the shape's own colour. The colours show in Multi channel. With "Repeated colours get new channels" ticked, each time the slices go round the colours again they get channels of their own.
• Angle: where the two points of the ball sit. 0° = top and bottom, 90° = left and right, anything between = slanted. The dial points at one of the points. Turn it, type the degrees, or middle-click it for 0°.
• Roundness: 100 % makes the slices thin at the edges and widest in the middle, like stripes on a real ball. 0 % makes every slice the same width. Anything between is a mix. Middle-click: back to 100 %.
• Rows with gaps: on a shape with a hole (a ring), Whole row wraps each row as one, gap included; Each piece wraps every piece of a row on its own.
• Each slice becomes its own shape: on OK, every slice turns into a shape of its own (plain notes), so you can move it by itself. Ctrl+Z gives the one shape back.

The angle is measured in the shape's own box, so a circle drawn as an oval still acts like a ball, and 45° runs corner to corner. On a drawn shape the slices follow the drawing itself, so with an even number of slices the middle border is a straight line and 90° is a mirror image top to bottom. Pasted notes have no drawing, so their slices follow the notes' own edge instead. Borders that run almost sideways step one key at a time: that's as fine as MIDI's keys go.

On a shape with [page:recolour|Recolour] ([page:stir|Stir], Shuffle, Per chord, Clumps...), [page:warp|Warp] or [page:liquify|Liquify], this can also change the colours of notes elsewhere in the shape, because those colours depend on the notes around them. In Single channel that can also change where overlapping notes end. To keep the colours as they are, use Turn into plain notes first.

## Your own default

The pin left of OK saves the page shown as what a new page starts with. Click it: Save current as default, or Forget my default. Black and leaning = none of yours saved, green and pointing down = saved. With a default saved, opening the window on a shape without this tool adds the page at once. Pages already made never change, and Reset still means "does nothing". Every saved default is listed in [page:settings#User set defaults|Settings → User set defaults], where you can change or reset it.
