Warp… (in the Recolour row, between Stir… and Liquify…) bends the colours inside a shape with a grid of points, the way the Warp tool of photo editing programs bends a picture. Drag a point on the piano roll and the colours around it follow like rubber. The notes themselves never move or change length: only which colour (channel) each note has changes. No colour is added or blended: every note takes the colour of the note the bend pulled into its place.

The grid is kept with the shape as points, never baked in. Come back any time and move them again; change the colours underneath (Recolour, Stir, Colours) and they flow along the same bend; move or stretch the shape and the bend goes with it. The colours bend in this order: Recolour, then every Stir, then the Warp, then the Liquify.

## Dragging the points

Open Warp…: the selected shape shows a grid over it. Drag any point and the colours change live while you drag, exactly as they stay when you let go. A point can go anywhere, also past the shape's edge. Each drag is one undo step (Ctrl+Z); a click without moving changes nothing. Esc while dragging puts the point back.

The window follows whichever shape is selected. With several shapes selected, each one shows its own grid. Pressing a shape away from its points picks it but never moves it while the window is open.

## Curve handles

Click a point and its handles show: small dots on the lines around it, plus the dot of each neighbour that faces it. The grid line between two points is a curve shaped by the two dots facing each other on it, like the pen tool's curves. Drag a dot to bend the line and the colours along it.
[clip:warp-handles]

Dragging a dot turns the one on the other side of the point too, so the line runs smoothly through the point. Hold Alt while dragging to move one dot alone and make a sharp corner there. Hold Alt while you press a corner point to make it smooth again. Right-click a dot you dragged to put it back; when all of a point's dots are back, it's smooth again. Moving a point carries its dots along. Mirror moves the twins' dots as mirror images too. Changing the grid size keeps the bend but puts the dots back.

## Picking several points

Ctrl+click points to pick several: a picked point is filled; Ctrl+click it again to drop it. Or drag a box from an empty spot inside the grid to pick the points in it (Ctrl+box adds to the ones already picked). Drag any picked point and they all move together, as one undo step; Mirror still moves their twins. The arrow keys nudge the picked points (Shift moves them ten times further); quick presses in a row are one undo step. Delete removes the lines through every picked point. A click on an empty spot of the grid, or Esc, drops the picks; the next Esc deselects the shape. The curve handles show only while one point is picked.

## Presets

Preset lays a ready-made bend onto the grid: Arc, Arc lower, Arc upper, Arch, Bulge, Shell lower, Shell upper, Flag, Wave, Fish, Rise, Fisheye, Inflate, Squeeze or Twist. Bend sets how strong it is, from -100 to 100 % (a minus number bends the other way); Across or Up turns it to run along the time or along the keys (the round ones, Fisheye, Inflate and Twist, have no way). The points and their curve handles are set for you on the grid as it is, so a small grid already shows the whole bend and a finer one follows it more closely. A preset is a starting point: drag a point or a dot afterwards and Preset shows Custom. Picking a preset again lays it again over your changes (Ctrl+Z brings them back). Picking a preset, changing Bend and changing the way are each one undo step; dragging the Bend word sideways changes it live. Changing the grid size of an untouched preset lays it again on the new grid.

## Grid size

3 x 3, 4 x 4 or 5 x 5 cells, or Custom… to set Across and Up apart (1 to 20 each), for example more cells across for a long, flat shape. Changing the size of a shape you already bent keeps its bend: the new points take it over as closely as they can. A bump a coarser grid can't make comes out softer, but going back to the size you started from gives the bend back exactly.

## Split

The grid's lines don't have to be evenly spaced. Click Across, Down or Both in the Split row, then click the grid on the piano roll: a new line goes through that spot (Both adds one each way). Nothing moves when you split: the new line follows the bend already there, so you can add more points just where you need them. The button lets go after one click; Esc or clicking it again lets go without splitting. With a Mirror picked, the mirrored line is added too.

Right-click the grid for the same choices at that spot (Split across here, Split down here, Split both ways here). Right-click right on a line for Remove this line. Or click a point and press Delete: the lines through it go (a point on the shape's edge loses only its inside line; a corner keeps both). Removing a line keeps the bend as closely as the fewer lines allow. A split grid shows Custom under Grid size; picking a ready size or typing a count spaces the lines evenly again.

## Cells and Colour edges

Cells: Smooth bends the colours like rubber; each point bends only the 4 cells around it. Straight cells bends each cell on its own, with sharp folds along the grid lines. Switching moves only the curve handles you never dragged: the ones you dragged stay where they are.

Colour edges: Sharp (the start) gives each note the colour of the nearest note, and is the quickest. Smooth makes the edges between colours follow a stretched bend more smoothly, and calms the colours where a cell is squashed. It is slower on big shapes. A note is one key tall, so a shallow edge is still a staircase of one-key steps either way.

## Folds

Drag a point past its neighbours and the grid folds over itself like paper: two parts of the colours lie in the same spot. Pulled part on top (the start) shows the part you pulled over; Pulled part underneath tucks it behind instead. The pulled part is the one whose points moved farther. Along the crease itself a thin line of mixed colours can stay.

## At the edges

What is pulled in where the bend reaches past the shape's edge, as in Stir: Escape, Wrap around, Repeat the colours (a colour cycle carries on past the edge with no seam), Bend or Cut.

## Mirror

Mirror moves a point's twin too while you drag, so both sides bend alike. Sideways and Up / down mirror across the grid's middle line, 4-way across both. Half turn moves the point opposite through the middle the opposite way, so a bend at 10 o'clock repeats at 4 o'clock.

The mirror lines (or Half turn's ring) are drawn over the grid, and the twins of the point under the mouse light up. A point on a mirror line only slides along it; in 4-way and Half turn the middle point (the cross) can't move. Picking a mirror changes nothing by itself: only your next drags are mirrored. Mirror is a drawing aid and isn't saved with the shape.

## Strength and Show the bend

In the Warp window, untick "Show the bend" to hide a shape's Warp without losing the grid; tick it again and it comes back the same. Strength (0 to 400 %) moves the colours less or further along the same bend: 0 shows none, 200 % twice as far. Dragging a point on a hidden Warp shows it again. Reset puts every point back and takes the Warp away.

## Big shapes

On shapes with millions of notes, letting go takes a moment while the notes are made. While dragging only the notes whose colour changes are worked out, so the grid stays live. 4-way and Half turn bend more of the shape at once, so they are a little slower.

## Edits that change notes

Some colours depend on the other notes in the shape: Stir, Warp and Liquify take the colour of the nearest note, Per chord and Per run follow their group, Shuffle changes a colour that would sound together with another, and Shuffle and Clumps count from the shape's first note and lowest key. So editing notes (Glue, Chop, Claw machine, Strum, Gate trim, Gate subtract, Gate extend, Sustain, Fill gaps...) can change colours elsewhere in the shape, and in Single channel where overlapping notes end. To keep the colours as they are, use Turn into plain notes first.
