Kestrel (GPU, Experimental) is a MIDI out that makes the sound on your graphics card instead of the processor. It can keep hundreds of thousands of notes ringing at once, so very dense MIDIs sound much fuller than they do through Built-in BASSMIDI. Pick it in Settings (Help menu) → Sound → MIDI out. It plays your soundfonts: the same stacked list as Built-in BASSMIDI (see Playback).

It needs 64-bit Windows or Linux and a fairly recent graphics card with an up-to-date driver. The first Play starts Kestrel, which takes a few seconds while it reads your soundfonts (a small "Starting Kestrel…" window). After that the soundfonts stay loaded while Kestrel is your MIDI out: changing its settings is quick, and changing the soundfont list reads only the soundfonts you added. Picking another MIDI out lets them go. If Kestrel can't start, or stops while playing, a window says why and offers Built-in BASSMIDI instead.

With a loaded MIDI, Kestrel reads its busiest parts ahead in the background, so walls of notes don't stutter. For a very big MIDI this can take a minute and a few GB of memory the first time. When it's done, Spiderweb asks whether to keep the read-ahead next to the MIDI's saved index: kept, the next load is ready in a moment.

Settings (Help menu) → Sound, while Kestrel is picked:
• Max voices: the most notes that can sound at once (300,000 to start; type 300k, 1m and so on). More = fuller busy parts, but each block takes the graphics card longer. If the card can't take that many, Spiderweb lowers it and says so.
• Lower it by itself when it can't keep up: ticked, the quietest notes are left out when the card falls behind, so playback never stops. Off, every note plays and it stutters where it can't keep up.
• Slow down to keep up (off at first): ticked, the song plays slower instead when the card can't keep up (down to 10 %, the pitch the same) and goes back to its own speed once it can. With "Lower it by itself" ticked too, notes are left out only once it's at the slowest, or once playing slower stops helping (very many notes ringing at once).
• Block size: how much sound is made at a time. Bigger = easier in busy parts, but the sound starts a little later after Play.
• Limiter and DC blocker: keep very loud parts from clipping and the bass from drifting, as in Kestrel's own renders.
• Graphics card: Automatic picks the strongest card (a laptop's own graphics card rather than the one built into the processor). Each card is listed twice, Vulkan and DirectX 12: two ways of talking to the same card. If one stutters or fails, try the other. If the picked card isn't there any more (another PC, a card turned off), Kestrel plays on the strongest one, the setting goes back to Automatic and the status line says so.
• Interpolation: how the soundfont's recordings are played higher or lower than they were recorded. Nearest = fastest, a bit grainy on high notes; Linear = Kestrel's usual; Cubic = smoothest, more work for the card.
• When voices run out: what happens when Max voices are all in use and a new note comes. Stop the quietest (Kestrel's usual: fading notes go first), Stop the oldest, or Skip new notes (what's sounding goes on).
• Release floor: how quiet a fading note gets before it's stopped (-100 dB to start). Higher, like -60, gives shorter tails, so fewer notes ring at once in busy parts.
• Velocity threshold: notes with a velocity under this aren't played at all (0 = every note). Dense MIDIs often hide many very quiet notes, so leaving them out saves a lot of work.

Advanced (click "▸ Advanced" to open it; leave these as they are unless you need one):
• Too many new notes at once: when more notes start in one block than there's room for, keep the loudest (Kestrel's usual) or spread evenly (every part of the block keeps some).
• Most stopped at once: the most of Max voices stopped for new notes in one block (25 % to start).
• Most layers a note: some soundfonts play several recordings for one note; this is the most played for each note (16 to start). Lower = less work, but such soundfonts may sound thinner.
• Filter, Vibrato and tremolo, Modulation envelope: the soundfont's own filters, wobbles and envelopes, all on at first, as in Kestrel's own renders. Off = a little less work, but the instruments may sound different than meant.

Changing any of them while playing starts Kestrel again from the play line, in a moment (the soundfonts aren't read again). Graphics card sends the soundfonts to the card again (a moment), and Filter reads them again (a few seconds: Kestrel uses the filters while it reads them).

When the card can't keep up, a red "Overloaded" shows at the right end of the status line (while "Lower it by itself" lowers the voices, with how many it lets sound now: "Overloaded: 470,000 of 1,000,000 voices", of your Max voices): lower Max voices, pick a bigger Block size, or tick "Lower it by itself" or "Slow down to keep up" (an orange "Slowed to 62 %" shows while it plays slower). Where the sound stutters, the play line waits with it, so the line always stays on what you hear.

31-EDO Audio plays through Kestrel too (see 31-EDO). For now, the Hz bass preview, the synth window and the live keys still play through Built-in BASSMIDI.
