Press Tab to swap the piano roll for the track list, and Tab again to go back. It shows the tracks your MIDI will have: one row per track, in the same order as Export MIDI and Export to Domino write them, each with a small picture of its notes in its channel's colour.

The All tracks row at the very top shows every track's notes together, each in its track's colour. It's taller than the tracks, so it doesn't look like one more track. It's highlighted while no track is: the piano roll then shows every track, and Spiderweb puts each new shape on channels itself (see Channels).

The Conductor row under it has a line at each tempo change: the faster the tempo, the taller the line, from this song's slowest tempo (lowest) to its fastest (tallest), so any tempo a MIDI file can hold fits. When the fastest is more than 4 times the slowest, the heights use the doubling scale, so one huge jump doesn't flatten the rest. Rest the mouse on it to see the BPM there. Short lines at its top mark time and key signatures.

A new project starts with one empty Track 1 and nothing highlighted: shapes you draw go into it, and Spiderweb adds more tracks when it needs them. Track 1 stays in the list (and in Export MIDI) even when it's empty. If you remove it, add a track by hand to get one back, or just draw: Spiderweb makes a track for the notes by itself.

Spiderweb picks the tracks for your shapes itself (see Channels): use the list to check which shapes land on which track before you export. You can also add tracks of your own (see below). Rest the mouse on a track's name to see its channel, how many notes it has and which shapes they come from. Long names end in "…" and show whole there.

Zoom and scroll with the scrollbars, like the piano roll: drag an end of the bar under the list to zoom in time, drag an end of the bar on its right to make the rows taller or shorter. Ctrl+wheel zooms in time, Ctrl+Shift+wheel changes the rows' height, Shift+wheel scrolls sideways. Zoomed far out, each column holds 2, 4, 8... bars, so the bar numbers never run into each other. The track list remembers its zoom and where you were, also after Spiderweb restarts. While playing, you can scroll freely: the list (and the piano roll) waits until you stop for half a second, then jumps back to the play line.

## Adding your own tracks

Right-click a track's name in the track list (or the empty space below the names):
• Add Track adds one empty track after the track you right-clicked (right-click below the tracks to add it at the end). It gets the lowest channel no track uses yet. When every channel is taken, it starts again from channel 1 (tracks can share a channel).
• Add Tracks… adds one track for each channel you tick. Drag over the ticks to tick or untick many at once.
• Remove Track removes a track and every note on it, like in Domino. A shape with colours on other tracks too keeps those: it becomes plain notes of the colours left, each still on its track. One of Spiderweb's own tracks goes once its notes are gone. A loaded MIDI's track is left out of the list and of Export MIDI (the track holding the song's tempo and signatures can't be removed).
• Properties… sets a track's name and channel. Double-clicking its name does the same. On one of Spiderweb's own tracks it first makes all of Spiderweb's tracks yours (as dragging one does). On a loaded MIDI's track, Export MIDI writes that track again with the new name and every event on the new channel; a track you leave alone is still copied exactly.

A new track is highlighted at once. Your tracks stay where you put them, among the tracks Spiderweb makes for your shapes, and are saved with the project. Export MIDI and Export to Domino write them with their names and channels. Each change can be undone with Ctrl+Z. On a loaded MIDI, Add Track on one of the file's tracks puts the new track right after it.

## Moving tracks

Drag a track by its name up or down: a line between two rows shows where it lands, and it goes there when you let go. Every selected track goes along (see Highlighting a track), and tracks with gaps between them land together, in their order. Press Esc before letting go to leave them where they were. Dragging past the top or bottom of the list scrolls it. Each move can be undone with Ctrl+Z.

Dragging one of Spiderweb's own tracks makes all of Spiderweb's tracks yours, empty ones too: each becomes a track you added, on the same channel, with its shapes laid on it. Only the order changes, like in Domino, and every track keeps its notes and channel. Shapes you draw later with All tracks highlighted get new tracks of Spiderweb's again. While Spiderweb's tracks hold a picture, a mask, a Hz bass, a Channel per strip shape or pasted notes with CC events, they can't be moved yet. A green tinted track you drag becomes yours too.

A loaded MIDI's own tracks can be dragged too, and your tracks can go above or between them. The track list and Export MIDI follow the new order, and each of the file's tracks is still copied exactly. The piano roll keeps drawing the file's tracks in the file's own order (a later track covers an earlier one), so what covers what stays the same while you edit; other players draw the exported file in its new order, so there it can differ.

## Tracks holding several channels

A track whose notes are on two or more channels gets a ▸ before its name. Click the ▸ to open it: one "Ch n" row per channel shows under it, slightly indented, each with its own picture in that channel's colour. Click the ▾ to close it again. Closed, the track's picture shows each note in its channel's colour.

• Click a Ch row and the piano roll shows only that channel. Drawing or pasting with it highlighted puts the notes on that channel of the track.
• Each Ch row has its own speaker. The track's speaker mutes or un-mutes all of its channels; while only some are muted, its icon shows that. Right-click a Ch row's speaker to hear only that channel.
• Drag a track (or a Ch row) onto the middle of another track and rest there for half a second: the row lights up, and letting go puts its channels into that track. Passing over a row without resting never does this, so a track never ends up mixed by accident. Into an open track, a short indented line shows where the channel lands among its Ch rows. Several picked tracks or Ch rows go in together.
• When a channel is already in that track, Spiderweb asks once for all of them: Merge (share the channel) or Move to a free channel. Merge is the default when it changes nothing you'd hear, else Move. Tick "Don't ask again" to keep your answer until Spiderweb closes.
• Drag a Ch row out between two tracks and it becomes a track of its own there. Several picked Ch rows become one track each. An empty track dragged in stays as an empty Ch row until you drag it out.
• Drag Ch rows up or down inside their track to change their order. The order is kept in the project only: each channel keeps its notes and its colour, and a MIDI file keeps no order inside a track.
• Right-click a track → Split into tracks: one track per channel, right where it was, in its Ch rows' order. Pick two or more tracks, right-click → Join into a mixed track: all of them go into the topmost one.
• Ctrl+click and Shift+click pick several Ch rows, like tracks. Tracks and Ch rows are never picked together. Right-click picked Ch rows → Remove channel to delete your notes on them.
• Properties… is greyed on a track holding several channels: one channel for the whole track would put all its notes on it.

A loaded MIDI's track works the same way, but its notes stay the file's. A Ch row dragged out becomes a part of that track: a row of its own that Export MIDI writes as a separate track holding only those channels, named like the track. It's instant at any size. Drag it back onto any row of the same track to put it back. Delete loaded notes and Extract take only the channels the highlighted row shows. Two different tracks of a loaded MIDI can't be joined yet, and a loaded MIDI's notes never go into your own tracks (Extract them first). Properties… and Remove Track are greyed on a part and on the track it came from until its parts are back.

Export MIDI writes a track holding several channels as it is, and its message names the ones you made that way, since some MIDI programs can't open such a file. Export to Domino and Copy to Domino write one Domino track per channel instead, because Domino needs one channel a track.

## Drawing into a track

Highlight a track, then draw or paste: the new shape is laid on that track, like pasting in Domino. Its notes play on the track's channel and take its colour, whatever the Channels setting is, and they never move to another track when they overlap other shapes. Two shapes in one track may overlap on the same keys, as in many MIDI programs.

• A shape with several colours goes downwards (with Multi channel): colour 1 on the highlighted track, colour 2 on the row below, and so on, whatever track is there (one of yours or one of Spiderweb's). Past the last row new tracks are added at the bottom, each on the channel after the one above (channel 10 skipped while it isn't used).
• A copy keeps its columns, like Domino's: shapes copied from tracks 1 and 5 and pasted with track 11 highlighted land on tracks 11 and 15. Paste from Domino keeps the gaps between its tracks too.
• Once a shape is laid, each colour stays on its track: adding or moving tracks later changes nothing, nor does moving the shape. More colours later carry on below its last track. With Single channel or As drawn, every colour plays on the shape's first track, and the tinted tracks Spiderweb added for its other colours leave the list and the exports (the first track's tip counts them); back in Multi channel the colours go back onto them.
• On Spiderweb's own tracks the shape is pinned: nothing already there moves, and later shapes may share those tracks. Spiderweb's automatic shapes never land on tracks you added.
• Tracks Spiderweb adds next to yours get a green tinted name; they go away by themselves when no shape uses them. Change one in Properties… to keep it as yours.
• With All tracks highlighted, a pasted shape that was laid in this project stays on its tracks, and a duplicate always does.
• Changing a track's channel in Properties… moves its shapes to the new channel.
• One of a loaded MIDI's tracks works the same: the shape goes into that track, on its channel (see Load MIDI).
• Pictures, masks, Hz basses, Channel per strip shapes and pasted notes with CC events can't be laid on a track yet: they keep their own channels, and the status line says so.

## Selecting notes in the track list

Drag on the tracks' pictures to draw a Select box over some tracks and some time, like in Domino. It holds the notes that start inside it. Its edges go to the nearest line of the track list's snap (Shift: free), so the play line, which the press puts there, sits on its start or end; Ctrl+drag adds another box, and a click outside the box or Esc drops it. Drag on the bar numbers instead to select that time on every track: the bar numbers turn black there and the tracks stay as they are. Drag that black part sideways to move its notes in time. Ctrl+A selects from the song's start to the last note's end the same way; the tracks' names don't turn blue, since blue names are for selecting tracks.

• Drag inside the box to move its notes to other tracks and in time. The box's left edge lands on the nearest snap line, and tracks keep their distance from each other. Hold Ctrl to move copies instead.
• A shape wholly inside the box moves as it is. A shape only partly inside is cut at the box's edge: both parts become plain notes, and the part inside moves.
• Moved notes stay on the tracks they land on: inside one of yours, pinned on one of Spiderweb's, drawn into one of a loaded MIDI's.
• A loaded MIDI's notes in the box become an Extract first (see Load MIDI), then move with the rest. Copies of them are plain notes and hide nothing in the file.
• Ctrl+C copies the notes in the box, Ctrl+X cuts them and Delete deletes them. Ctrl+V pastes at the play line, starting from the highlighted track and going down, like in Domino; a box goes round what came in.
• Copy to Domino (Ctrl+Shift+C) copies only the notes in the box while a box is there.
• A click inside the box changes nothing: the play line and the highlighted track stay. Right-click the box for Cut, Copy, Paste, Delete, Copy to Domino and Select all. Right-clicking the pictures outside the box drops it; Paste and Select all still work there.
• Every change is one step for Ctrl+Z. Pictures, masks, Hz basses, Channel per strip shapes and pasted notes with CC events can't be moved here: move them on the piano roll.

## Highlighting a track

Click a track to highlight it. A click also puts the play line there, on the track list's snap. Pressing Tab into the track list scrolls it to the highlighted track and the play line when they're out of view, and pressing Tab back does the same for the play line on the piano roll. Press Tab and the piano roll shows only that track's notes, its name in the piano roll's top left corner. Every other track is hidden until you go back to All tracks: click the All tracks row, press Esc in the track list, pick All tracks in the Multiple tracks ▾ list on the toolbar, or Ctrl+wheel up past the first track. The corner then says "All tracks". Shapes whose notes are all on hidden tracks hide too: their lines don't show and can't be clicked, Ctrl+A leaves them out, and Slice and Lasso don't cut them. Hidden notes still play and still go into the files.

Select several tracks like files in a folder: Ctrl+click more tracks to select them along with the highlighted one, Shift+click to select every track from the last one you clicked to this one, Ctrl+Shift+click to add those to what's selected. Selected tracks turn a fainter blue, and the highlighted track keeps its deep blue (the piano roll still shows that one). Ctrl+click a selected track again to drop it. A plain click or All tracks clears the selection. Right-click the name of one of the selected tracks and the menu works on them all: Remove Track removes them all, and Properties… is greyed while more than one is selected.

When a shape you draw, paste or move lands on another track, the highlight moves to that track, so it never vanishes under the mouse (if it lands on several tracks, every track shows). When channels are handed out again (for example a shape drawn over others), the highlight stays with the music it was showing.

## Multiple tracks

The Multiple tracks button on the toolbar shows other tracks faintly behind the highlighted one: on = the tracks you picked show too, off = the highlighted track alone. Its ▾ list picks them: the track before it, the track after it, Pick tracks…, or every track of the same colour. All tracks at its top highlights the All tracks row. Each track keeps its own picks, like in Domino: highlight another track and you see the tracks picked for that one.

Picked tracks get a dot in their colour in the track list. Show multiple tracks… (above the list, or Pick tracks… in the ▾ list) opens the list of tracks: click a track to pick it or not, drag to do the same to every track you cross. Faint sets how strongly they show (0 % hides them).

## Mute

Click a track's speaker to mute it: it isn't played until you click it again, and its picture turns grey. Drag from a speaker up or down to mute (or hear again) every track you cross. Right-click a speaker to hear only that track; right-click it again to hear every track. Muting only changes what you hear: Export MIDI and Export to Domino still write the track. When an edit changes which track a shape lands on, the mute (and a track picked to show behind) goes with that music; if its music is gone, it's cleared.

## Snap

The track list has its own snap (Bar at first), set with the toolbar's Snap while the track list shows; Tab back and Snap shows the piano roll's again. It's where a click puts the play line. Clicking the bar numbers with Shift held puts it exactly where you click.

Most shortcuts do nothing while the track list shows, so nothing changes out of sight. These work: Ctrl+A, Ctrl+C, Ctrl+X, Ctrl+V and Delete on the track list's Select box (see above), Space (play), Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y (undo / redo), Ctrl+S (save), Ctrl+N and Ctrl+O (new and open project), Ctrl+E (Export MIDI). The menus at the top work too.
